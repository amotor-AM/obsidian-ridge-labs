### Echo Chamber's public listing and source disagree: resolve versions first

The parent research agent verified [Echo Chamber's public US App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/echo-chamber-ai-transcription/id6761675060) on 2026-09-26. It showed version **1.3**, dated **September 10**, whose release notes describe free, unlimited recording/transcription/search and announce the Mac app. Listing body copy also promotes Pro with unlimited recording length and import plus AI. Listed compatibility included iOS 18 and macOS 15.1 on M1-class Macs; displayed Pro prices were $2.99 monthly, $29.99 yearly, and $79.99 lifetime.

That is verified **public listing evidence**, not a runtime examination of the store binary. Current source metadata says iOS 1.4/build 14 and enforces a different quota policy plus a per-session cap. Therefore **do not automatically replace live free-plan claims with HEAD's 60-minute allowance**, or replace the live model description with HEAD's Cedar options. First map the advertised build to its source/release notes and determine what the founder intends to publish next. The internal disagreement between listing body and release notes also needs resolution. This ledger identifies the conflict; it does not choose a commercial policy.

## Decisions that matter before a rewrite

1. **Echo Chamber's current-source offer differs substantially from the website.** Free now includes 60 recording minutes plus three imports, three AI chats, and three AI notes per calendar month. Pro bypasses quotas, but a recording still has a two-hour safety cap. “Unlimited recording length” and Pro-exclusive import are not accurate descriptions of this source.
2. **Echo Chamber's “bundled Bonsai 1.7B fallback” is stale.** Current provider choices are Apple Intelligence, optional Cedar 4B/8B downloads, and Off. Speech also routes between Apple Speech, Parakeet, and a low-memory Whisper fallback. One universal “Parakeet does everything” description hides real hardware/OS differences.
3. **Do not promote an older-device Vault fallback from backend files alone.** Vault contains Cedar routing, but its current root view blocks the product unless Apple Intelligence is available. The website's Apple Intelligence requirement is supported. Its `iOS 17` minimum is not: current build metadata specifies iOS 26.
4. **Mettle's private iCloud path is the Release default.** The audited source does not establish a user-facing opt-in switch. Calling it “optional iCloud” without explaining the default is misleading. It uses Apple's private database, not a studio training database.
5. **The three products have stronger concrete propositions than “private AI.”** Echo Chamber keeps the recording connected to the useful material made from it. Mettle turns an explanation or a constraint into a change to the training plan. Vault helps assess a purchase against current obligations and lets someone bring statements without connecting a bank.

## Echo Chamber

### Most supportable sales argument

**Keep the conversation, then use it.** The recording becomes a searchable transcript, a readable copy, notes, answers, and documents on the device. The differentiating experience is the continuity from what was actually said to the work someone needs afterward. Privacy makes it possible to use this on the conversations that matter; it should not replace explaining what the app does.

The best three to five benefits to develop, after matching them to the released build:

- Be present in the conversation, with live transcription and bookmarks as a return path.
- Go from a recording to readable text, notes, answers, and useful exports without making an AI-service upload.
- Bring recordings already made elsewhere; current source includes a limited free import allowance.
- Choose local intelligence appropriate to the device instead of assuming Apple Intelligence is the only path.
- On the native Mac product, capture meeting-app audio and surface meeting recording controls without sending a bot into the meeting. Keep this platform-specific and release-qualified.

## Mettle

### Most supportable sales argument

**A coach that can explain the plan and change it with you.** Tell it what happened, what equipment is available, or why a movement does not work for you. The app remembers the relevant constraint, proposes a change you can review, and grounds future prescriptions in logged training. This is a stronger story than an unexplained “adaptive AI program.”

Strongest benefits:

- Know the reason for the next load, rep range, and recovery week.
- Change session length, training days, split, or an exercise through a coach conversation; review the proposed change before applying it.
- Carry forward training constraints and preferences instead of repeating them each time.
- Spend less of a workout operating the phone: wrist controls, supported-motion rep detection, spoken cues, rest alerts, and resumable sessions.
- Bring previous workout records and take every set away as CSV; the source keeps history portable without making it Pro-only.

## Vault

### Most supportable sales argument

**Know what a purchase changes before you make it.** Bring receipts or statements, account for bills and income, then test the purchase against the money already committed. The local product is useful without a bank connection. Optional bank syncing saves entry work, but it carries a different boundary and belongs in its own clearly priced choice.

Strongest benefits:

- A purchase check grounded in current records, obligations, and goals, with an insufficient-information state instead of fabricated certainty.
- Receipt, CSV, and PDF import—including OCR for scanned PDFs—without making bank linking the price of admission.
- Review extracted transactions and retain corrections so repeated statement work can get easier.
- Turn a financial question into a concrete, inspectable next step, with deterministic numbers and an optional paid conversational layer.
- Keep a portable, passphrase-encrypted backup and ordinary CSV exports instead of relying on a studio account to recover access.
