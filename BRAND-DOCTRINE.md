# Obsidian Ridge Labs — Brand Doctrine

The governing document for every word this company publishes: website, App Store listings, release notes, journal posts, help articles, support replies, screenshots, social, and anything a contractor or a model writes on our behalf.

This file is not a style guide. A style guide tells you how to sound. This tells you what to believe, who to fight, what to repeat, and what to refuse — and then how to sound.

**When a drafting decision is unclear, the six laws decide, in order.**

---

## 0. How to use this file

1. Read **§1 The premise** and **§2 The locked decisions**. Those two sections are the whole brand compressed.
2. Find your surface in **§10 Page briefs**. Every page has an assigned law, an assigned line, and a required close.
3. Draft.
4. Run **§12 The pre-publish checklist** before anything ships. The one-line test kills most drafts on its own.

Change §2 in one place only: here, deliberately, with the founder present. Everything else in this file is downstream of it, and so is every sentence on the site.

### Full names are mandatory

Always write **Echo Chamber** and **Obsidian Ridge Labs** in full whenever naming the app or the company. Never shorten either name, including after the first mention. No nicknames, initials, truncated names, or shortened wordmarks.

This rule applies to website copy, navigation, logos and wordmarks, buttons, captions, accessibility labels, metadata, structured data, App Store copy, documentation, proposals, and conversation. Available space is a layout problem; it is never a reason to abbreviate a name.

## 1. The premise

> People are not shopping for software. They are shopping for a worldview, and they will take it from whoever states it best.

Belonging is not a nice-to-have. Baumeister & Leary (1995) put it next to food and safety as a basic human need. We are pack animals, and a pack forms around a way of seeing, a thing to fight, a mark to wear, a thing to do, a set of words, and someone willing to say what happens next. That is the entire mechanism behind Apple, Patagonia, Hermès, CrossFit, Lululemon, and the 40,000 people who fly to Omaha every year for a shareholder meeting.

### Congregation, not community

We do not build a community. A community is a Slack channel nobody opens. A **congregation** is a growing group of people who cannot unsee what you showed them and who describe themselves differently because of it.

The difference in practice:

| Community | Congregation |
|---|---|
| A place to post | A way to see |
| Members | Believers |
| Leaves for 10% off | Argues with strangers on your behalf |
| Asks for features | Asks what you stand against |
| You moderate it | You give it language |

Every piece of copy is judged by which one it builds.

### The six laws, in the order they must be earned

1. **The Lens** — hand people new eyes
2. **The Devil** — name what you fight
3. **The Symbol** — make belonging visible
4. **The Ritual** — give behavior a shape
5. **The Language** — coin the words
6. **The Prophet** — state the future like it already happened

You earn each law in order. A prophet without a lens is a man with a cardboard sign on a busy street.

## 3. Law 1 — The Lens

### What the law says
Jobs did not sell a faster box. He sold new eyes: a world where a computer is an instrument of creativity rather than a grey appliance that does arithmetic quickly. Once you wear those eyes, the grey box is offensive. Fishwife did it to a tin of fish. Nest did it to a thermostat and sold for $3.2B in cash three years later. The product is never the point. **The way of seeing is the product.**

### Our lens

Most people think privacy means giving something up: fewer features, a weaker model, a small sad life spent offline.

**Here is what is actually true: privacy is what makes software personal.**

A model in a data center has met a hundred million people, which is another way of saying it has met no one. It can only ever be as smart as the slice of yourself you were willing to paste into a text box on someone else's computer. An app that keeps your recordings, your receipts, your pages, and your people in the room is the only kind that can ever know the whole of you.

On-device is not the smaller option. It is the only one that goes all the way.

### The lens formula

Say this in public, repeatedly, until people finish the sentence for you.

> **Most people think ___. Here's what's actually true: ___.**

The working set:

- Most people think private AI means weaker AI. Here's what's actually true: an AI that can't be trusted with your whole life can never actually know you.
- Most people think privacy is about hiding. Here's what's actually true: privacy is the precondition for being known — by the people, and the software, you choose.
- Most people think free AI is free. Here's what's actually true: intelligence rented by the month is paid for with your inner life. That is The Trade.
- Most people think their notes, receipts, and voice memos are small data. Here's what's actually true: that is the complete map of a life, and it is worth more than the app that collected it.
- Most people think the cloud is where the intelligence lives. Here's what's actually true: it is already in your hand, in a chip you paid for, sitting idle.

### Rules
- The homepage states the lens in the first screen. Product pages express it through a specific use of personal context and the actual processing boundary. Lead with the app's category and benefit; do not repeat the homepage's argument. No throat-clearing or "in today's world."
- The lens is aspirational, not fearful. Protection is a feature. **Being known without being exposed** is the dream we are actually selling. If a page only frightens, it is half-built.
- Hero line of record, and the home page H1: **"AI that knows you. Not one that watches you."**
- Studio line, for bios, the README, and anywhere a sentence has to describe the company rather than the promise: **"Apps that mind their own business."**

## 4. Law 2 — The Devil

### What the law says
Apple made IBM into Big Brother, then made the PC a man in a cheap brown suit — and barely talked about Apple at all. Patagonia's devil is mindless consumerism, and it told you not to buy the jacket. Muñiz & O'Guinn (2001) found that brand communities hold together around a shared adversary, and their headline example was Mac versus PC. A customer leaves you for a coupon. A follower defends you in the comments.

Two hard rules from the law: pick a **behavior, a belief, or a broken status quo** — never a person. And never dilute the fight. You cannot build a congregation by kind-of-sort-of-maybe opposing something that feels a bit like a problem.

### Our devil: The Trade

> **The Trade is the quiet deal behind almost every "free" AI app: your conversations, finances, journals, habits, and relationships become the fuel, and the company rents the intelligence back to you by the month.**

It is not a company. It is not Apple. It is not "the cloud" as a technology — a CDN is not a moral failure. It is a **business model that standardized itself into invisibility.** Our job is to name it so people can point at it.

### The refusal line

> We exist because the normal way of building software is broken: your life as the fuel. We refuse The Trade.

### Rules
- Name The Trade by name. A paragraph that fights it without saying "The Trade" is diluted. Rewrite it.
- **Banned dilutions:** "that may be a fair trade for some work," "it depends," "both approaches have tradeoffs," "we're not saying the cloud is bad." We state our side. The boundary documentation states the facts. Those are different jobs and they never blend.
- Name The Trade where the studio argues its position. Product conversion moments explain the app's value, evidence, requirements, and offer. Do not insert another manifesto into every product hero or price section.
- We never punch at people, at other developers, or at Apple. Apple is our platform and our silicon. The enemy is the deal, never the counterparty.
- Competitor names appear only in the journal, only in a comparison, only with sources, and only about architecture — never as villains.

## 5. Law 3 — The Symbol

### What the law says
White earbuds on a subway car. The yellow band. The Stanley cup. A symbol is a belief made visible, and it recruits with no ad spend because it starts conversations you are not in the room for. Research on brand prominence (2010) found visible markers signal group membership and change how people are treated. The mistake is treating merch as revenue instead of recruiting.

### Our symbols

1. **The cut** — the slash accompanying the full Obsidian Ridge Labs name. One shape, one meaning: *the line nothing crosses.* Die-cut sticker, black-on-black or Signal Green, for laptops and cases. Also our social avatar mark. When the mark includes the company name, all three words must appear.
2. **Signal Green `#c7ff3e`** — the color of the local path. Teaching rule for insiders: **green means it stays on your side of the glass.** Use green consistently for on-device states in UI, diagrams, and marketing art. Believers learn to read it. Outsiders just feel the consistency.
3. **The shelf** — every app icon shares the obsidian tile and the green signal line, so the collection reads as one shelf on a home screen. Spot a believer across a screen.
4. **Phrase-symbols** — "Local by design." and "On your side of the glass." Words travel further than logos and they convert on contact. These are the wearable words: bios, footers, status chips, release-note signatures.

### Rules for a digital-first brand
Nobody can wear an app. So we give people things to **put on other things** and **say out loud**: stickers in every launch kit, the cut as an avatar, the phrases as bios. Merch is a recruiting tool. It is never a revenue line, and we never open a store.

## 6. Law 4 — The Ritual

### What the law says
Belief usually follows behavior, not the other way around. Apple turned a product launch into an annual mass. Hermès turned waiting into an initiation. Aronson & Mills (1959): people who went through an effortful entry liked the group more than people who walked in. Vohs et al. (2013): performing a small ritual before eating made a plain carrot taste better and nearly doubled what people paid for chocolate — but only when they did it themselves.

The ritual should help someone make an informed choice. The cited psychological studies do not establish that a forced detour improves software conversion. Explain the boundary where the choice happens and let people inspect it further.

### Ritual 1 — The Boundary Check

Our owned, repeatable behavior. Before you trust **any** AI app, ask three questions:

1. **Where does the processing happen?**
2. **Where does the storage live?**
3. **What connects to the network, and when?**

> An app that cannot answer in one breath isn't private. It's packaged.

We invite people to run it on us first, and then to go run it on everything else on their home screen. That second half is the entire point: it works as advice about apps we did not make, which is what makes it ours. Competitors fail it in public, and we never have to name one.

### Ritual 2 — The Airplane-Mode Test

> Turn on airplane mode. Whatever still works is yours. Whatever dies was a rental.

Apply this invitation to a named app and verified workflow after its required setup. Model downloads, purchases, trusted-time checks, sync, and selected connected features have separate requirements. Do not imply that every feature in every app works without a connection.

### Ritual 3 — The threshold

**Put the boundary beside the decision.** Product pages provide concise processing, storage, and connection facts near the installation or preview action, with the full explanation linked. A direct App Store action is available for a released app. Development apps offer a real preview and requirements, never a fictional download or waitlist.

## 7. Law 5 — The Language

### What the law says
Real brand communities run on shared consciousness, shared rituals, shared responsibility — and shared language at the center (Muñiz & O'Guinn, 2001). Apple has fed us language for decades: *Think different. A thousand songs in your pocket. There's an app for that. One more thing.* CrossFit does it harder: WOD, the box. Know the words and you are in; don't and you are a tourist. Lifton (1961) called the cult version **loading the language**: tidy in-group terms that quietly do your thinking for you.

The common failure is coining a phrase with no belief under it. Superhuman tried to own "inbox zero," but the phrase was already public and meant nothing new, so it never stuck to them. **A phrase has to carry a worldview.** And naming is claiming: name it and you own it.

### The canon

Priority order. These are the lines we want repeated back to us.

1. **Each page earns its own close.** The former universal closing slogan is retired from public pages, shared footers, and repeated product sections. Close on the app's useful next action.
2. **"The Trade."** — the devil's name. Two words, always capitalized, never softened.
3. **"AI that knows you. Not one that watches you."** — the lens, verbatim. The home page H1.
4. **"Apps that mind their own business."** — the studio, in five words. Bios and the README.
5. **"Privacy is what makes it personal."** — the lens, spoken form.
6. **"Local by design."** — the badge. Status chips, footers, bios.
7. **"Run the Boundary Check."** — the call to ritual.
8. **"Turn on airplane mode. Whatever still works is yours."** — the test.
9. **"On your side of the glass."** — the symbol phrase for anything local.
10. **"Personal AI is not coming. It is already in your hand."** — the prophecy.

Already-owned phrases that keep working. Keep repeating them:

- "Data has gravity."
- "The cloud must earn its place."
- "Offline is the test."
- "Your data belongs to you."
- "Convenience is not consent."
- "A useful transcript should not create a second audience."
- "The strongest privacy feature is data that never leaves."
- "Your life is not raw material."

### Renamed things (naming is claiming — keep the capitalization)

- **the boundary** — what can and cannot leave the device
- **the Boundary Check** — the three questions
- **the collection** — the full product shelf. Never "our suite," never "our portfolio."
- **the standard** — the Obsidian Ridge Labs standard: local core, offline-ready, every connection named
- **the path** — local vs. cloud processing route
- **the cut** — the slash glyph
- **the shelf** — the collection as it appears on a home screen

### The banned list

These are why machine-made copy reads machine-made. None of them appear in our work.

- **Brochure-speak:** seamless, effortless, empower, unlock, elevate, supercharge, unleash, harness, leverage, streamline
- **Inflation:** cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changing, next-generation, best-in-class, robust, holistic, innovative, "solutions"
- **AI tells:** journey, landscape (figurative), realm, delve, dive into, tapestry, "in today's world," "it's not just X, it's Y," "imagine a world where"
- **Performed emotion:** we're passionate, we're excited, thrilled, amazing, incredible
- **Self-praise adverbs:** clearly, carefully, thoughtfully, deliberately, simply, and *just* as a minimizer
- **Congregation-killers:** "community" (we build a congregation), "users" (we serve *people*, or "the person doing the work")
- **Meta-signposting:** never announce your own virtues. "Explained plainly," "honest boundaries," "proof not posture," "straight answers," "no fluff." A brand that keeps insisting it is honest reads like a witness rehearsing a defense. Be plain. Don't file a permit for it.

### Voice mechanics

- Declarative sentences. Subject, verb, object. Then stop.
- One long sentence has to be earned by short ones around it.
- Rhythm beats symmetry. Kill the machine tricolon habit ("X. Y. Z." in three equal beats). Allow it rarely, when the beats are genuinely different lengths.
- Concrete beats abstract, always: "recordings, receipts, journals, relationships," never "your data points."
- Second person. The reader is in the sentence.
- Verbs over nouns. "Nothing joins the call" beats "no meeting-bot integration."
- No sentence exists to introduce the next sentence.

### The one-line test

> **Could any competitor in this category have written this sentence?**

If yes, cut it or sharpen it until the answer is no. This single test removes more slop than the entire banned list.

## 8. Law 6 — The Prophet

### What the law says
Jobs walked on stage only after the lens, the enemy, the symbols, the rituals and the language were already in place — and then stated the future as fact. Research on the confidence heuristic (2012) found people read confidence as competence, because when there is no proof yet, certainty is the only signal available. Founders hedge: *I think this might probably be where things are going.* A prophet states the future like it already happened.

### Our prophecy

Say these without flinching. Never attach "we think," "maybe," or "someday."

- **Personal AI is not coming. It is already in your hand.**
- The next decade of personal computing is won on the device, not in the data center.
- Every year the cloud asks for more of your life. Every year the device needs less of the cloud.
- We are not building smaller cloud apps. We are building the end of The Trade.

### The tension rule (memorize this)

**Prophetic in the pulpit, precise in the spec sheet.**

- Mission, manifesto, hero: zero hedges. The future, stated.
- Product pages, privacy model, FAQ, App Store metadata: exact numbers, exact conditions, labeled estimates, "in development" wherever it is true.

Prophecy never touches a fact. That separation is exactly what makes the prophecy believable — and it is the only reason we are allowed to preach at all.

## 11. Writing for AI visibility and search

Models and search engines now read this site more often than people do, and the same rules that make copy quotable make it retrievable. These two goals do not conflict — generic copy loses at both.

**Structure**

- One `h1` per page, stating a position, not a topic.
- `h2`s state a concrete benefit or ask a useful question. The headings alone should communicate the page’s argument. Questions are appropriate for FAQs; do not impose them on every section.
- Answer in the first sentence under the heading, then expand. Never build to the answer.
- Facts belong in lists, tables, and `dl` pairs. Retrieval systems lift structured blocks cleanly and lose them inside paragraphs.
- Every claim that can be checked gets a number, a condition, or a source next to it.

**Entities**

Always name **Obsidian Ridge Labs** and **Echo Chamber** in full, on every mention. For other entities, use the complete name at least once per page: *Apple Intelligence*, *Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3*, *Bonsai 1.7B*, *FSRS*, *Plaid*, *iCloud*, *Apple silicon*, *iPhone, iPad, Mac*.

**Quotability**

A quotable sentence is a retrievable sentence. Short, declarative, self-contained, no antecedent required. "Turn on airplane mode. Whatever still works is yours." survives being lifted out of the page with zero context. "This approach ensures a more private experience" does not survive anything.

**Honesty as a ranking asset**

State release status every time. Label estimates as estimates. Give the conditions under which a number was observed. Publish a performance number only when a reproducible evaluation supports that exact app, build, model, hardware, and task. The former Echo Chamber word-error-rate claim is unsupported and must not return as an example or benchmark. A model vendor's result is not an app result.

**Per-page requirements**

- Title under 60 characters where possible, leading with the entity or the position.
- Meta description: the answer, not a teaser. One sentence, under 155 characters, no "learn more."
- FAQ blocks: real questions, answered in 40–80 words, first sentence complete on its own.
- Schema: `SoftwareApplication` on product pages, `FAQPage` where FAQs render, `BreadcrumbList` everywhere below root, `creativeWorkStatus` accurate on every app.
- `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` stay in sync with the collection. Every app, every status, no exceptions.

**What we never do for traffic**

Keyword stuffing, invented statistics, fake test results, "review" language for unreleased software, or comparison pages that hide the release status of our own product. Every one of those is a dilution of the devil fight, and it costs more than it earns.

## 12. The pre-publish checklist

Nothing ships until every line is true.

1. **The one-line test.** Could a competitor have written any sentence here? Delete or sharpen it.
2. **The first screen earns attention.** The homepage states the lens. A product page names its category, useful outcome, and actual local-processing boundary.
3. **The devil is named.** If the page fights The Trade, it says "The Trade."
4. **No dilution.** No "may be a fair trade," no "it depends," no both-sides paragraph.
5. **Banned list, zero hits.** Search the draft for: seamless, effortless, empower, unlock, elevate, journey, robust, cutting-edge, community, users, thoughtfully, carefully, simply.
6. **No meta-signposting.** The copy never announces its own honesty, clarity, or plainness.
7. **Every section adds something.** No repeated studio sermons or recycled product closes.
8. **The next action is real.** Installation for a released app; an accurate preview for a development app. No compulsory manifesto detour.
9. **Tension rule holds.** Prophecy in the mission copy, exact facts in the spec copy, no bleed in either direction.
10. **Status is accurate.** Shipping is "on the App Store." Everything else is "in development," stated plainly, every time.
11. **Concrete nouns.** At least one list of real things — recordings, receipts, journals, relationships — not "your data."
12. **Read it out loud.** If you run out of breath, the sentence is a paragraph wearing a disguise.
13. **Full names, every mention.** Echo Chamber and Obsidian Ridge Labs are never abbreviated in visible copy, wordmarks, accessibility text, metadata, or supporting documentation.

## 13. Channel applications

**App Store listings.** Lead with the app’s specific benefit and category. Explain the feature that earns the promise, then the offer and requirements. Match the published binary. Do not force a contrast formula or repeat the homepage.

**Release notes.** Never "bug fixes and performance improvements" alone. Plain declaratives about what changed, and sign the ritual when it is true: "Search now finds words in the middle of a sentence. Airplane mode unchanged, as always."

**Journal.** Every post carries the lens. Dev logs are boundary logs: here is what we refused this sprint and why. Titles state positions, not topics — "Why we build exclusively for Apple," never "Some thoughts on platform choice." Comparisons cite primary sources and state our own release status first.

**Support.** Declaratives and ownership. "You're right. Here's the fix." Never "we apologize for any inconvenience." A real person signs it.

**Social.** The lens formula is the content format: "Most people think ___. Here's what's actually true: ___." Repeat The Trade until the team groans. That groan is the signal from §9, and the correct response to it is to double down.

**Art and OG images.** Signal Green means local, everywhere, with no exceptions. OG cards carry a canon line, not a feature. Alt text describes the image as rendered.

## 14. Research findings and product evidence — approved September 26, 2026

The founder approved the research-led proposal in `docs/research-2026-09-26/RECOMMENDATION.md`. Supporting source audits cover all ten app projects. The research brief and provenance are in `outputs/obsidian-ridge-redesign-research.md` and its `.provenance.md` sidecar.

### What the evidence supports

- Descriptive headings and concise, factual writing help people scan and use a page. Usability improvements are not automatically conversion improvements.
- Public reference websites show design and sales structure; their private conversion rates are unknown. Raycast, Family, Chronicle, Linear, and Nothing are references, not a conversion leaderboard.
- Observational landing-page benchmarks do not establish a causal lift for this audience. Do not promise a percentage improvement or import another industry’s target.
- Product demonstrations should connect the promise to an actual result. Attractive motion must preserve loading speed, control, reading, and reduced-motion access.
- The homepage owns the studio argument. The app owns its category problem. A consistent belief does not require identical copy or page order.

### Claim standard

Maintain the app, supported claim, exact source, version/build, platform, entitlement, caveat, and verification status for each material product assertion. Distinguish **implemented in source**, **configured**, **publicly advertised**, and **runtime/release verified**. An unused class, fallback behind a blocking gate, test fixture, README, or local StoreKit catalog is not proof of a shipping feature.

Current source and a live listing can disagree. In that case resolve the release mapping before publishing exact commercial claims; use the supported common description in the meantime. Do not expose internal audit paperwork as marketing copy. State meaningful customer requirements and limitations where they affect the choice.

Do not invent testimonials, measured retention or strength gains, transcription accuracy figures, speed claims, universal compatibility, encryption guarantees, or autonomous behavior. A model prompt is not a guarantee of factual output. Local inference and local-only storage are separate claims. Document automatic connections as well as optional ones.

### Material findings to preserve

- As of October 3, 2026, Memora is available on the App Store at https://apps.apple.com/us/app/memora-study-flashcards/id6810065777, confirmed by the founder and the public listing. Echo Chamber and Memora are released; the other eight apps remain in development. Memora is free to download with optional Plus and requires iPhone with iOS 26 and an A17 Pro chip or later. Current features and local prices should follow its App Store listing and in-app offer.
