# Obsidian Ridge Labs — Brand Voice

The operating manual for every word this company publishes: website, App Store listings, release notes, journal posts, support replies, screenshots, and social.

It is built on six laws of belief — the mechanics behind Apple, Patagonia, Lululemon, Hermès, CrossFit, and every brand people defend for free — and on the underlying research: belonging as a basic human need (Baumeister & Leary, 1995), brand community markers (Muñiz & O'Guinn, 2001), initiation severity (Aronson & Mills, 1959), ritual and consumption (Vohs et al., 2013), the confidence heuristic (2012), brand prominence and signaling (2010), and Lifton's "loading the language" (1961).

The premise, stated once and never re-litigated:

> People are not shopping for software. They are shopping for a worldview, and they will adopt it from whoever states it best. "Most people think X. Here's what's actually true." Say it in public, over and over, until people repeat it back to you — then double down.

The six laws, in the order they must be earned:

1. **The Lens** — hand people new eyes
2. **The Devil** — name what you fight
3. **The Symbol** — make belonging visible
4. **The Ritual** — give behavior a shape
5. **The Language** — coin the words
6. **The Prophet** — state the future like it already happened

A brand earns each law in order. A prophet without a lens is a cardboard sign on a busy street.

## Law 1 — The Lens

### What the law says
Steve Jobs didn't sell a faster box; he sold new eyes — a world where a computer is a tool of creativity, not calculation. Fishwife sells canned fish as a little luxury. Nest sold a thermostat as a design object. The product is never the point. The *way of seeing* is the product.

### Our lens
Most people think privacy means giving something up — fewer features, less power, a small sad life offline.

**Here's what's actually true: privacy is what makes software personal.** A cloud AI knows everyone, so it knows no one. It can only ever be as smart as the slice of your life you're willing to leak to a server. An app that keeps your recordings, receipts, journals, and relationships *in the room* is the only app that can ever know the whole of you. On-device isn't the smaller option. It's the only real one.

### The lens formula (use it in public, repeatedly)
Fill in the blank and publish. Repeat until people finish the sentence for you.

- Most people think private AI means less capable AI. Here's what's actually true: an AI that can't be trusted with your whole life can never actually know you.
- Most people think privacy is about hiding. Here's what's actually true: privacy is the precondition for being known — by the people, and the software, you choose.
- Most people think "free" AI is free. Here's what's actually true: intelligence rented by the month is paid for with your inner life. That's The Trade.
- Most people think their notes, receipts, and voice memos are small data. Here's what's actually true: that is the complete map of a life, and it is worth more than the apps that collect it.

### Rules
- Every flagship page states the lens within the first screen. No throat-clearing.
- The lens is aspirational, not fearful. Protection is a feature; *being known without being exposed* is the dream we sell.
- Hero line of record: **"AI that knows you. Not one that watches you."**

## Law 2 — The Devil

### What the law says
Apple painted IBM as Big Brother, then PC as the man in the cheap brown suit. Patagonia's devil is mindless consumerism, not a competitor. A shared adversary turns customers into followers: a customer leaves for a 10% coupon; a follower defends you in the comments. Two rules from the law: pick a *behavior, belief, or broken status quo* — never a person — and never dilute the fight with "kind of sort of maybe."

### Our devil: The Trade
**The Trade is the quiet deal behind almost every "free" AI app: your conversations, finances, journals, habits, and relationships become the fuel, and the company rents intelligence back to you by the month.**

It is not a company. It is not Apple. It is not "the cloud" as a technology. It is a *behavior* — a business model that standardized itself into invisibility. Our job is to name it so people can point at it.

The refusal line (the "we exist because" formula):
> We exist because the normal way of building software is broken: your life as the fuel. We refuse The Trade.

### Rules
- Name The Trade by name. A paragraph that fights it without saying "The Trade" is diluted. Rewrite it.
- Banned dilutions: "that may be a fair trade for some work," "it depends," "both have pros and cons." We state our side; the boundary documentation states the facts.
- The devil gets sharpest at conversion moments: hero, manifesto, pricing, App Store descriptions.
- We never punch at people, developers, or Apple. The enemy is the deal, not the counterparty. (Apple is our platform, not our enemy.)

## Law 3 — The Symbol

### What the law says
White earbuds on a subway car. The yellow Livestrong band. The Stanley cup. A symbol is a belief made visible, it recruits without ad spend, and the mistake is treating it as merch-for-revenue instead of merch-for-recruiting.

### Our symbols
1. **The cut** — the slash in OBSIDIAN/RIDGE. Die-cut slash sticker (black on black, or Signal Green) for laptops and phone cases. One shape, one meaning: the line nothing crosses.
2. **Signal Green** (`#c7ff3e`) — the color of the local path. Teaching rule for insiders: *green means it stays on your side of the glass.* Use green consistently for anything on-device in diagrams, UI states, and marketing graphics. Believers learn to read green = local; outsiders just feel the consistency.
3. **The app tiles** — every product icon shares the same obsidian-dark tile and the green signal line, so the collection reads as one shelf in the App Store. Spot-a-believer across a screen.
4. **Phrase-symbols** — "Local by design." and "On your side of the glass." Words travel further than logos; these are the wearable words.

### Rules for a digital-first brand
Our customers can't wear an app. So we give them things they can *put on other things* and *say out loud*: stickers in every launch kit, the slash as a social avatar mark, the phrases as bios and release-note signatures. Merch is a recruiting tool, not a store.

## Law 4 — The Rituals

### What the law says
Behavior comes before belief. Apple's keynote is a yearly mass; Hermès turned waiting into an initiation; the harder the door, the more people love the room (Aronson & Mills, 1959). Small pre-consumption rituals measurably raise perceived value (Vohs et al., 2013). Zero friction means zero investment, and zero investment means zero devotion.

### Our rituals
1. **The Boundary Check** — our owned, repeatable behavior. Before you trust *any* AI app, ask three questions:
   1. Where does the processing happen?
   2. Where does the storage live?
   3. What connects to the network, and when?
   If an app can't answer in one breath, it isn't private — it's packaged. We invite people to run the Boundary Check on us first. This is congregation-building disguised as consumer education, and competitors fail it in public.
2. **The Airplane-Mode Test** — turn on airplane mode. Whatever still works is yours. Whatever dies was a rental.
3. **The threshold** — the standard comes before the software. The privacy model is not an appendix; it is the front door. Product pages link the boundary *before* the install button. We keep one earned step between curiosity and commitment.

### Rules
- Publish the Boundary Check as a standalone page and as graphics. It must work as advice for apps we didn't make — that's what makes it ours.
- Never remove the threshold to "reduce friction." Friction with meaning is initiation; friction without meaning is bugs. We keep the first and kill the second.

## Law 5 — The Language

### What the law says
Real brand communities run on shared consciousness, shared rituals, shared responsibility — and shared language at the center (Muñiz & O'Guinn, 2001). CrossFit's WOD. Apple's "one more thing." Lifton called cult usage "loading the language": in-group terms that quietly do your thinking. A coined phrase must carry a worldview, or it never sticks (Superhuman's "inbox zero" belonged to everyone, so it stuck to no one). Naming is claiming: name it and you own it.

### Our scripture (repeat until people quote it back)
The canon, in priority order:

1. **"Move the intelligence. Not the private life."** — the law of the house. Close every major page with it or its sibling.
2. **"The Trade."** — the devil's name. Two words, always capitalized.
3. **"AI that knows you. Not one that watches you."** — the lens, verbatim.
4. **"Local by design."** — the badge. Status chips, footers, bios.
5. **"Run the boundary check."** — the call to ritual.
6. **"Privacy is what makes it personal."** — the lens, spoken form.
7. **"On your side of the glass."** — the symbol phrase for anything local.

Already-owned phrases to keep repeating (found in the wild on our own pages — they work):
- "Data has gravity."
- "The cloud must earn its place."
- "Offline is the test."
- "Memory belongs to you."
- "Convenience is not consent."
- "Your AI. Your phone. Your business."
- "A useful transcript should not create a second audience."
- "The strongest privacy feature is data that never leaves."

Renamed things (naming is claiming — use the capitalization):
- **the boundary** — what can and cannot leave the device
- **the collection** — the full product shelf, never "our suite"
- **the standard** — the Obsidian standard: local core, offline-ready, connections named
- **the path** — local vs. cloud processing paths
- **the cut** — the slash glyph

### The banned list
These words are why the old copy felt machine-made. None of them appear in our copy:

- **Brochure-speak:** seamless, effortless, empower, unlock, elevate, supercharge, unleash, harness, leverage
- **Inflation:** cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changing, next-generation, best-in-class, robust, holistic, innovative, solutions
- **AI-slop nouns and verbs:** journey, landscape (metaphorical), realm, delve, dive into, discover the world of, tapestry, elevate
- **Performed emotion:** we're passionate, we're excited, thrilled, amazing, incredible
- **Self-praise adverbs:** clearly, carefully, thoughtfully, deliberately, simply, just (as minimizers)
- **Congregation-killers:** "community" (we are not building a wasteland in Discord; we are building a congregation), "users" (we serve *people*, or "the person doing the work")
- **Meta-signposting:** never announce your own virtues. "Explained plainly," "honest boundaries," "proof, not posture," "straight answers" — a brand that keeps insisting it's honest reads like a witness preparing a defense. Be plain. Don't file a permit for it.

### Voice mechanics
- Declarative sentences. Subject, verb, object. Then stop.
- One long sentence must be earned by short ones around it.
- Rhythm beats symmetry. Kill the three-beat tricolon habit ("X. Y. Z." in machine rhythm) — allow it rarely, when the three beats are genuinely different lengths.
- Concrete beats abstract, always: "recordings, receipts, journals, relationships" — never "your data points."
- The one-line test before publishing: **could any competitor in our category have written this sentence?** If yes, cut it or sharpen it until the answer is no.

## Law 6 — The Prophet

### What the law says
Jobs walked on stage only after lens, enemy, symbol, ritual, and language were in place — and stated the future as fact. People read confidence as competence when there is no other proof yet (confidence heuristic, 2012). Founders hedge: "this might probably be the future." The prophet states the future like it's already here — and only earns that right after the first five laws.

### Our prophecy
Say these without flinching. Never attach "we think," "maybe," or "someday":

- **Personal AI is not coming. It is already in your hand.**
- The next decade of personal computing will be won on the device, not in the data center.
- Every year the cloud asks for more of your life. Every year the device needs less of the cloud.
- We are not building smaller cloud apps. We are building the end of The Trade.

### The tension rule (memorize this)
**Prophetic in the pulpit, precise in the spec sheet.**
- Mission, manifesto, and hero copy: zero hedges. The future, stated.
- Product pages, privacy model, FAQ, App Store metadata: exact numbers, exact conditions, labeled estimates, "in development" stated where true. We never let prophecy touch a fact. That is what makes the prophecy believable.

## Audit — where the previous copy stood

Scored against the six laws. Kept what worked; called out what didn't.

| Law | Verdict | Evidence |
|-----|---------|----------|
| Lens | **Half-built.** The fear side was strong ("The glass house is burning"); the aspiration was missing. Nothing told people what the world looks like *through our eyes* — only what to fear. | Rebuilt around "privacy is what makes it personal." |
| Devil | **Unnamed.** "The surveillance economy" was present but polite and faceless. Worse, the copy diluted its own fight: "That may be a fair trade for some work." The transcript is explicit: you cannot kind-of-sort-of-maybe fight the devil. | Named: The Trade. Dilutions removed. |
| Symbol | **Dormant.** A wordmark and a green accent existed, but with no story. Nobody could explain what the green means or what to repeat to a friend. | Signal Green = the local path. The cut. Phrase-symbols. Sticker plan in Law 3. |
| Rituals | **Absent.** No repeatable behavior, no earned threshold, no test people could run on us or competitors. | Boundary Check, Airplane-Mode Test, install threshold. |
| Language | **Scattered treasure.** Several genuinely owned phrases existed ("Data has gravity," "Convenience is not consent") but were never systematized — and the surrounding copy was full of AI tells: self-praise adverbs, meta-signposting, uniform paragraph rhythm. | Canon locked, banned list enforced. |
| Prophet | **Missing.** Not one declared future on the whole site. "Remains in active development" appeared in every product description; "should not" hedging everywhere. No one stood on the stage. | Prophecy written; tension rule added so facts stay exact. |

Specific fixes made in the rewrite:

- **Hero:** "Pure privacy. Intelligence on your terms." (generic superlative, could be any privacy brand) → "AI that knows you. Not one that watches you." (the lens, ownable)
- **Download page:** claimed "eight" apps in development — there are nine. Generic copy smell: numbers nobody counted. Fixed, and the page now ends on the ritual.
- **"Great apps should not require another subscription"** on a page selling three subscription tiers → replaced with ownership framing ("Own your transcripts. Choose your term.").
- **"Local intelligence, thoughtfully made"** (footer) → "Local by design." (self-praise adverb → owned phrase)
- **Manifesto:** kept its best line ("The glass house is burning"), replaced the hedge with The Trade, and gave it a prophetic coda.
- **Kept, because they were already right:** "Move the intelligence. Not the private life." / "Your AI. Your phone. Your business." / "A useful transcript should not create a second audience." / "The strongest privacy feature is data that never leaves." / "Data has gravity." / all four countermeasures / every FAQ answer (factual, specific, conversational — the least generic copy on the site).

## Applying the voice everywhere

**App Store listings.** Subtitle = proposition in the "X, not Y" form ("A strength coach that shows its work"). First line of the description = the lens for that category. Facts after the belief. No "introducing," no "welcome to."

**Release notes.** Never "bug fixes and performance improvements" alone. State what changed in plain declaratives and sign the ritual when true: "Search now finds words in the middle of a sentence. Airplane mode unchanged, as always."

**Journal.** Every post carries the lens. Dev logs are countermeasure logs: here is the boundary, here is what we refused this sprint. Titles state positions, not topics ("Why we build exclusively for Apple," not "Some thoughts on platform choice").

**Support.** Declaratives and ownership. "You're right. Here's the fix." Never "we apologize for any inconvenience."

**Social.** The lens formula is a content format: "Most people think ___. Here's what's actually true: ___." Repeat The Trade until the team groans — that is the transcript's own signal that you are just starting to say it enough.

**OG images and art.** When og-v2.png is regenerated, it carries the new hero line and Signal Green. Alt text always describes the image as rendered.
